Teaching

I view my role as an educator as someone who passes along the knowledge we gain as a species to the next generation of curious young minds and equips them with the tools they need to continue this inquiry. Curiosity drives us a species, and sharing our knowledge allows us to build upon the discoveries of our ancestors. I decided to pursue a PhD because I am someone who has always been curious about the universe we inhabit, and I'm incredibly fortunate that there are generations of scientists and teachers that came before me who sought answers and passed their knowledge along to the younger generations. To that end, I am passionate about teaching and sharing the knowledge I have gained with others so that the pursuit of knowledge can persist long after my time here on this planet.

Past Courses (all at Rutgers)

  • Instructor of Record for Astronomy and Cosmology 109 (online at Rutgers Newark, Spring 2023)
    • Introduced astronomy and cosmology concepts to non-physics majors. Taught both a lecture and a lab, both online. Covered topics including radiation, light, astrophotography, Kepler's Laws, Stars, Supernovae, Dark Matter, and Hubble's law, and included many hands-on activities, digital demos (especially NAAP Labs), and metacognition activities.
  • Course Administrator for Extended Analytical Physics I (Fall 2024)
    • This course teaches first-year mechanics concepts to students with no Calculus background. Administrator responsibilities included overseeing the weekly TA meetings, making recitations, quizzes, and solutions, and reserving classrooms for exams and instructor meetings. Also ensured that students with disabilities had proper accomodations.
  • TA for Intro to Physics Research 155 (Fall 2024)
    • A course designed to introduce first and second year undergraduate students from underrepresented backgrounds to physics research. During the first half of the course, the students learned coding skills and during the second half they were paired with a faculty or graduate student mentor and applied their skills to complete a research project.
  • Recitation TA for Extended Analytical Physics I (Fall 2023)
    • First semester physics course taught to students with no background in Calculus. Covered kinematics, Newton's laws, circular motion, friction and gravity, and basic fluid dynamics.
  • Recitation TA for General Physics 203 (Summer 2022)
    • Introductory physics course covering foundational mechanics concepts such as motion, gravitation, momentum, energy, fluids, thermodynamics, and waves. As a recitation TA, I went over example problems and helped students as they worked through problem sets in groups.
  • Recitation TA for Analytical Physics II 227 (Fall 2021)
    • Introductory physics course covering foundational electricity, magnetism, and simple circuits. As a recitation TA, I went over example problems, and helped students as they worked on problem sets.
  • Lab TA for General Physics Lab 205 (online, Fall 2020 and Spring 2021)
    • Introductory physics lab covering kinematics, newton's laws, circular motion, momentum, energy, fluids, and thermodynamics. Taught online via Zoom, as they worked on virtual lab set ups using the PhET simulations.
